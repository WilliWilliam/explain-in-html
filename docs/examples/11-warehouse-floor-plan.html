How a container is deconsolidated at a bonded warehouse

Colombia · sea imports · for people new to foreign trade · fictitious case, companies and plates

A consolidated container carries goods from several owners under a single master transport document, issued to the freight forwarder. Inside, each importer's cargo has its own house document (documento hijo). At the bonded warehouse (depósito habilitado) the container is opened and every package is counted against its house document. From there, each shipment follows its own process: its own declaration, its own DIAN review and its own payments. That is why the shipments arrive together in the container and leave on different days.

One container, several ownersThe master belongs to the freight forwarder. Each importer declares with its house document and answers only for its own cargo.
What was counted has to match the papersIf a package is missing or extra, DIAN is notified and only that shipment is held until it is cleared up.
The first to get customs release leaves firstThe order inside the container does not matter. For each declaration, DIAN decides whether it gets automatic release (levante) or a documentary or physical inspection.

  1. 1 · Arrives at the gate

    The container leaves the port (here, Cartagena) for the warehouse the freight forwarder chose. At the gate they check that the container number and the seal number match the transfer form.

    The transfer form (planilla de envío) is generated in DIAN's systems and authorizes moving the cargo, not yet cleared through customs, from the port to the warehouse. Nobody has seen the cargo yet: the container is still closed.

    What gets recordedTransfer form · container MODU 000123-4 · seal 000481 · destination: Example Warehouse

  2. 2 · To the unloading dock

    The container backs up to the dock door. They confirm the seal arrived intact, break it and open the doors. The warehouse records that it received the container.

    If the seal arrives broken or with a different number, that is recorded before opening. It changes who is liable if something turns out to be missing later.

    What gets recordedReceipt form (planilla de recepción) · MODU 000123-4 · seal 000481 intact · 6 Oct 2026, 10:40

  3. 3 · Deconsolidate: separate and count

    The packages come out and go into one lane per house document. Each shipment is counted against its document. A, B and C match. For D, only 2 of the 3 packages arrived.

    The count is package by package, with weight and marks. Besides shortages, they look for overages and damaged packages, and everything is written down.

    Before moving on: one package from D is missing. Does that also hold up A, B and C?

    No. Each house document is a separate process. The shortage only affects D; the other three shipments carry on as normal.

    What gets recordedCount · A 4/4 · B 2/2 · C 3/3 · D 2/3 (1 missing)

  4. 4 · Report, then to the racks

    DIAN is told what arrived and what did not match. Each shipment goes to its place on the racks, still not cleared through customs. The empty container goes back to the shipping line.

    The unloading and discrepancy report (informe de descargue e inconsistencias) for the house documents is filed by the freight forwarder. The storage period starts running: one month from arrival in the country, extendable by one more month.

    What gets recordedUnloading and discrepancy report · house BL D · shortage: 1 package

  5. 5 · Each importer declares

    Each owner, through its customs broker (agencia de aduanas), files its own import declaration and pays its duties and taxes. DIAN decides, separately for each declaration, how much to check.

    A: automatic customs release. B: physical inspection, the packages have to be opened. C: documentary inspection of the invoices and of the INVIMA prior approval. D: has to clear up the shortage before declaring.

    Question: based on what you see in the floor plan, which shipment leaves first?

    See answer

    A. It has automatic release and waits for no review. Whether it was at the back or by the door of the container does not matter.

    What gets recordedDeclarations · A automatic release · B physical inspection · C documentary inspection · D not declared

  6. 6 · A leaves first

    A has its release. With storage fees paid and the house document released by the freight forwarder, the warehouse books A's truck and loads its 4 packages through door A.

    Without the freight forwarder's release, which comes only after it collects its local charges, the warehouse does not deliver, even if DIAN has already granted customs release.

    What gets recordedPickup order · house BL A · 4 packages · truck plate XYZ 001

  7. 7 · Physical inspection of B

    A's truck leaves. B's packages move to the inspection area: a DIAN officer opens them and compares the goods with what was declared. C is still in document review.

    While the inspection lasts, the storage period is suspended. The cargo does not leave the area without DIAN's authorization.

    What gets recordedInspection record · B's declaration · 2 packages opened · compliant

  8. 8 · B and C leave

    B's inspection comes out compliant and it gets release. C's documents are approved and it gets release too. Both go to the dispatch dock and onto their trucks.

    B and C leave at the same time even though their reviews were different. What lines them up is the release date, not where they sat in the container.

    What gets recordedRelease · B and C declarations · trucks plate XYZ 002 and XYZ 003

  9. 9 · D leaves last

    D cleared up the shortage and declared the 2 packages that did arrive. It gets release and its truck loads through door D. The missing package is handled separately if it turns up.

    How a shortage is justified, and who is liable for it (the shipping line or the freight forwarder), is worth asking your customs broker.

    What gets recordedD's declaration · 2 packages · release · truck plate XYZ 004

  10. 10 · Close-out

    The four shipments left on four trucks, at three different moments. They arrived together in one container, but each left when its own process was done.

    What gets recordedDeconsolidation closed · 11 packages delivered · 1 shortage reported

Why one shipment leaves before another

To the shipping line the container is a single shipment, but not to customs. For DIAN what counts are the house documents, and each one moves at its own pace. These are the things that decide when each shipment leaves:

The deadlines, with real dates example

Suppose the vessel arrives in Cartagena on Tuesday 6 October 2026. Some deadlines are counted in business days and others in calendar months, and there are public holidays along the way.

  1. Tue 6 Oct 2026The vessel arrives and the unloading report for the master is filed.
  2. Wed 14 Oct 2026Last day to take the cargo to the warehouse: 5 business days at a seaport. Monday 12 October is a public holiday (Día de la Raza), so it does not count.
  3. Fri 6 Nov 2026The one-month storage period at the warehouse ends, without extension. That is 21 business days for the whole process, because Monday 2 November (All Saints' Day) is also a holiday. An inspection pauses the count.
  4. Sun 6 Dec 2026End of the one-month extension, if requested. It falls on a Sunday: ask whether it moves to the next business day.
  5. AfterwardsA shipment with no release and no re-export falls into legal abandonment. Getting it back costs more than declaring it on time would have.

Deadlines per articles 169 and 171 of Decree 1165 of 2019, as summarized in DIAN rulings from 2025. Decree 659 of 2024 changed part of these rules, and some depend on DIAN switching on its systems. Confirm them with your customs broker before relying on them.

Cases outside the walkthrough

  • Overage. A package shows up that is not on any house document. It is reported just like a shortage and held until someone proves it is theirs.
  • Damage. A package arrives broken or wet. It is noted and photographed during the count, before it is moved, because that is the evidence for an insurance claim.
  • Broken or different seal. It is recorded before opening. From then on, liability for any shortage is argued differently.
  • Dangerous or refrigerated cargo. It needs special areas, and not every warehouse accepts it. That is settled before choosing the warehouse.
  • Pre-declaration cargo check. DIAN can examine the cargo before the declaration. While it lasts, the deadlines are suspended.
  • An importer who does not show up. Its cargo stays on the racks while the others leave, and its clock keeps running toward legal abandonment.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Thinking the container leaves as a whole. It leaves by house document. Ask about your client's cargo, not about the container.
  • Mixing up the master and the house document. The importer declares with its house document. The master belongs to the freight forwarder.
  • Believing that customs release means you can pick up. You still need storage fees paid, the freight forwarder's release and the truck appointment.
  • Declaring what the invoice says instead of what was counted. If there was a shortage, you declare what arrived, once the discrepancy is cleared up.
  • Requesting prior approvals after the cargo arrives. INVIMA or ICA approvals are processed beforehand, in the VUCE. Otherwise the cargo waits in the warehouse, racking up storage fees.
  • Counting everything in business days. The transfer to the warehouse is counted in business days, but the storage period is one calendar month.

Questions for the expert

For the customs broker

  • From what exact date do you count this cargo's one-month storage period, and what happens if it ends on a Sunday or a holiday?
  • Is it worth filing an advance declaration so the release comes through as soon as the cargo arrives?
  • How is a shortage justified, and can we declare only what arrived?

For the bonded warehouse

  • How much do you charge per day for storage, and from when do you start charging?
  • How is the truck appointment booked, and what documents does the driver have to bring?

For the freight forwarder

  • Who files the discrepancy report for the house documents, and within what deadline?
  • What local charges have to be paid for the house document to be released?

Glossary

Consolidated container
A container with cargo from several owners (LCL), put together by a freight forwarder.
International freight forwarder
Consolidates and deconsolidates. Holds the master BL and issues the house documents.
House document (documento hijo)
Each importer's BL or air waybill within the master. It is the unit the importer declares with.
Cargo manifest
List of everything the vessel is carrying, which the shipping line transmits before arrival.
Bonded warehouse (depósito habilitado)
A warehouse authorized by DIAN to store cargo that has not yet been cleared through customs.
Transfer form (planilla de envío)
Authorizes moving the cargo from the port to the warehouse.
Unloading and discrepancy report (informe de descargue e inconsistencias)
Report to DIAN of what arrived extra, short or damaged.
Import declaration
The document with which the importer clears its goods through customs and assesses the taxes.
Risk management
DIAN system that decides whether a declaration gets automatic release or goes to inspection.
Customs release (levante)
DIAN's authorization to dispose of the goods.
Prior approval (visto bueno)
Prior permit from another agency (INVIMA, ICA) for certain products, processed through the VUCE.
Legal abandonment (abandono legal)
What happens when the deadline passes with no release: the goods are placed at DIAN's disposal.
