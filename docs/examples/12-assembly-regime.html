Motorcycle assembly regime in Colombia

Yes, it exists. An assembler authorized by the Ministry of Commerce (MinCIT) imports motorcycles knocked down (CKD kits) without paying customs duty (arancel) or VAT (IVA) on arrival. It stores and assembles them in a warehouse the DIAN licenses for that purpose. It pays once the motorcycle is finished: a 3 % duty, and only on the imported parts each motorcycle contains, versus 30–35 % for a gasoline motorcycle imported fully built. In return, every year it must buy a minimum share of parts made in Colombia.

Reading time
It is a real customs procedureIt is called “import for transformation and/or assembly” (importación para transformación y/o ensamble, Decreto 1165 de 2019, art. 250). For motorcycles, MinCIT runs it as the “Motorcycle Assembly Regime” (Régimen de Ensamble para Motos).
You pay at the end, and littleThe parts come in without paying. When the finished motorcycle leaves, you pay 3 % on the value of its imported parts, not on the whole motorcycle.
Two authorities, two permitsMinCIT authorizes the company and monitors the minimum of Colombian parts. The DIAN licenses the warehouse and receives the declarations.

    Who can use it and which permits it needs

    There are two permits and they go in order: without MinCIT's you cannot apply for the DIAN's.

    1. MinCIT authorization as an assembler. You apply to the Foreign Trade Directorate (Dirección de Comercio Exterior) through the VUCE, module “Régimen de Ensamble para Motos de Transformación o Ensamble – RTE” (Decreto 1131 de 2026, art. 2.2.1.10.3.2). Attachments include, among others: capital structure and shareholders, plant location and area, projected production and sales for three years, the local parts incorporation program, the letter of intent from the parent company or the CKD suppliers, the commitment to after-sales service and spare parts for at least ten years after the model is discontinued, and a pre-feasibility study. MinCIT decides within at most 90 calendar days. The authorization lasts five years, renewable for five more; for new companies, three years (art. 2.2.1.10.3.5).
    2. Licensing the warehouse with the DIAN as a private warehouse for transformation and/or assembly (Decreto 1165, art. 88; Decreto 1131, art. 2.2.1.10.2.11). In the same resolution that licenses the warehouse, the DIAN authorizes the company to declare under the procedure. You submit MinCIT's decision and a description of the process, with the subheadings that come in and the products that go out (Resolución DIAN 46 de 2019, art. 255).

    After that you must meet the PIN every year and report it to MinCIT through the VUCE: the first-half report by July 31 at the latest and the annual report by March 1 at the latest, the latter with the Form B sheets for domestic motorcycle parts and certification by the statutory auditor (revisor fiscal), accountant or auditor (Decreto 1131, art. 2.2.1.10.2.12).

    The minimum of Colombian parts (PIN)

    PIN = value of local parts ÷ (local parts + CIF value of the imported CKD) × 100. Only local purchases with at least 40 % domestic value added count as local. It used to be a flat 17 % for all motorcycles. With Decreto 1131 de 2026 it depends on the type of motorcycle and rises every year:

    How much duty is paid, and on what

    The rule is in art. 252 of Decreto 1165. The ordinary declaration is filed when the motorcycle is finished. Duties and taxes are assessed on the value of the foreign parts it contains, at the rate of the subheading of the unit produced. For motorcycles assembled by an authorized company, that subheading is 98.01 of the Customs Tariff (Arancel; Note 2 of chapter 98: the levy “se liquidará por unidad producida o ensamblada dentro del depósito habilitado o en zona franca”, i.e. is assessed per unit produced or assembled in the licensed warehouse or free trade zone). The value of the imported parts comes from the production cost sheet.

    Two sources disagree
    * Decreto 1432 de 2025 sets 35 % for the five combustion motorcycle subheadings (8711.10 to 8711.50). Its art. 4 says it takes effect 15 days after publication (Diario Oficial of December 26, 2025). The compiled Tariff table in the DIAN normograma still shows 15 % and 30 % on those lines, with a note dating the change to January 2027. Confirm the current rate with your customs broker before quoting. Art. 3 of the decree preserves FTAs: with preferential origin the duty may be lower.

    How it differs from Plan Vallejo and the free trade zone

    All three let you import without paying on arrival. Two things change: where the product has to go, and when and on what you pay.

    To be confirmed
    Note 2 of chapter 98 says per-unit assessment under 98.01 applies “dentro del depósito habilitado o en zona franca” (in the licensed warehouse or in a free trade zone). Art. 483 says what leaves a free trade zone pays the rate of the final product's subheading. We found no official source explaining how the two combine for a motorcycle assembler located in a free trade zone. Ask the DIAN before deciding where to locate the plant.

    What changed in 2026

    • Decreto 218 de 2026 (in force since March 17, 2026). Sets the time limit inside the warehouse: 15 business days to declare under the procedure; after that, as long as production of the final good lasts plus two more months. It also allows national or nationalized goods from third parties into the warehouse, identified and kept separate.
    • Decreto 1131 de 2026 (in force since August 21, 2026). Replaces the single 17 % PIN with three rising categories. Creates subheadings 8711.60.00.20 and 9801.20 for electric motorcycles and the 0 % rate conditional on 20 % electric production. Moves the procedures to the VUCE RTE module. If it is later verified that the company did not meet the 20 % or the PIN, it must pay the duty under Decreto 1881 de 2021, plus penalties (paragraph 2 of art. 2.2.1.10.2.4).

    Open questions

    • PIN for the rest of 2026. The new table starts in 2027 and replaced the 17 % article. We found no transition rule for the rest of 2026. Ask MinCIT.
    • 15 business days or 15 days. Decreto 1165 (art. 88, amended in 2026) says business days. Resolución DIAN 46 (art. 258) says “quince (15) días” (fifteen days). The decree prevails, but it is wise not to wait until the last day.
    • Entry declaration. Decreto 659 de 2024 created the “declaración de ingreso” (entry declaration) for goods entering a warehouse for transformation and/or assembly. Part of its operation depends on the DIAN certifying its IT services (art. 67 of that decree). Check whether it already applies to you before including it in your procedure.

    Typical beginner mistakes

    Mistake and correction
    MistakeCorrection
    Believing a warehouse is enough to pay less duty.First the MinCIT authorization, then the DIAN licensing of the warehouse. Without both, the parts pay their normal duty (8714: 10 %).
    Thinking the 3 % is paid when the kit is imported.Nothing is paid on import. The 3 % is paid when each finished motorcycle is nationalized, on its foreign parts.
    Leaving finished motorcycles in the warehouse “until there is an order”.There are two months from when the final good is obtained. If the deadline passes without declaring, exporting or re-exporting, the goods are deemed abandoned to the State (la Nación, art. 252).
    Counting any local purchase toward the PIN.Only parts that physically form part of the motorcycle and have at least 40 % domestic value added count, backed by Form B and the national production registry.
    Mixing local parts and imported kits on the floor.They must be identified by physical or electronic means and kept separate from the goods under the procedure (art. 85 par. 2 and art. 88).
    Sending subassemblies to an outside workshop without permission.Moving goods out of the warehouse requires authorization from the local DIAN office (Dirección Seccional) and coverage by the guarantee (Res. 46, art. 259).

    If the motorcycles are exported, the procedure also ends with the export or re-export (Decreto 1165, arts. 252 and 380), without paying the import duty. The warehouse requires a guarantee. For the first year it is 1 % of the customs value of what is projected to be stored; on renewal, 1 % of what was stored the previous year, with reductions if there are no penalties (art. 109, num. 4).

    Glossary

    CKD
    Completely knocked down: a motorcycle imported fully disassembled, with the minimum degree of disassembly in Note 2 of ch. 98: unpainted body and frame; engine, suspension, brakes and wheels separate.
    PIN
    National Integration Percentage (Porcentaje de Integración Nacional): how much of the motorcycle's material was bought in Colombia, versus the imported CKD.
    Licensed warehouse
    Depósito habilitado: a warehouse the DIAN authorizes to store and assemble foreign goods that have not yet paid duties and taxes.
    Nationalize
    Declare and pay duties and taxes so the goods are freely available and can be sold in Colombia.
    Restricted disposal
    Disposición restringida: the goods cannot be sold or freely used until the procedure is completed.
    Customs transit
    Tránsito aduanero: permission to move foreign goods that are not yet nationalized from the port to another licensed place in the country.
    VUCE
    Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (Foreign Trade Single Window). It hosts MinCIT's RTE module for authorizations and reports.
    Heading 98.01
    Special Tariff heading under which motorcycles assembled in the country by authorized companies are nationalized.
